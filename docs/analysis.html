Northwind CRM case / Analysis

Why paying customers dropped while signups grew

Northwind CRM · Self-serve funnel · Data Jan 1 – Jun 30, 2026

Paying customers dropped 47% QoQ after Free CRM launched (316 → 168 per month), even though signups grew 25% QoQ. Signup-to-paid conversion by SKU didn't change. What changed is the SKU mix: Free CRM became the primary call-to-action across the site on Apr 1 and now takes 69% of signups, but only 4.7% of them become paying customers, vs ~29% for Starter and Pro trials. The shift in SKU mix accounts for the drop.

Data

We used 307,715 website sessions, 7,338 signups and 1,495 closed opportunities. Sessions and signups cover Jan 1 – Jun 30, 2026; opportunities run to Jul 26 so June signups have time to close. Signups are grouped by the month they signed up (cohorts) and compared as Q1 (Jan–Mar, before Free CRM) vs Q2 (Apr–Jun, after launch) using monthly averages. A paying customer is an opportunity with amount > $0 closed within 30 days of signup. How the data was validated, cleaned and joined is in Data methodology.

Problem

Northwind CRM shows an apparent signup-to-paid conversion problem: blended conversion fell from 29% to 12% and paying customers dropped ~47% QoQ (316 → 168/month), even as signups grew 25% QoQ. The timing aligns with the launch of a new packaging scheme that includes a freemium plan.

Hypotheses

  1. Free CRM is cannibalizing Starter and Pro (SKU mix shift). People who used to start a Pro or Starter trial now get Free CRM. If Free CRM converts at a lower rate, it could be pulling down the blended conversion rate.
  2. Signup quality deteriorated (top of funnel). Lower-quality signups could also explain the drop in conversion, regardless of which SKU people sign up for.

What we found

  1. The SKU mix shifted; signup-to-paid conversion by SKU didn't. Starter went from 81% of signups to 19%, Pro from 19% to 12%, and Free CRM took 69%. Signup-to-paid by SKU stayed flat (Starter 28% → 29%, Pro 34% → 29%†).
  2. The drop is the same across every top-of-funnel trait. Channel, entry page, device, new vs returning visitor, company size and country all fall 12–22pp QoQ in signup-to-paid conversion, all in April, after a flat Q1. Free CRM takes 65–74% of Q2 signups in every slice. Signup quality doesn't explain the drop.
  3. Net signup growth is much smaller than Free CRM's volume. Starter and Pro trial signups dropped by 660/month QoQ and Free CRM grew by 932, netting out at +271 signups/month QoQ. That lift could be attributed to Free CRM, but not conclusively*.
  4. Free CRM upgrades go only to Starter. All 132 Free CRM upgrades were to Starter ($35); none went to Pro. All happened within 29 days of signup.
  5. Traffic growth came almost entirely from low-converting sources. Sessions grew 44% QoQ, and 98% of the new sessions came from Display and Direct. Those two channels brought only 43% of the new signups: Display turns fewer than 0.5% of visits into signups, and Direct's rate fell from 2.9% to 1.9% as its volume nearly doubled. This affects traffic efficiency, not the quality of the signups (finding 2).

* 271 net new signups/month is an upper bound for what Free CRM added. In the same period traffic grew 44% QoQ (mostly Display and, in June, Direct) and the signup increase is spread across all channels. With this data we can't separate how much of the growth came from Free CRM vs traffic.

† Pro's Q1 average is lifted by January (38.6%). Feb–Jun Pro ranges 26–32%, in line with Q2.

Recommendations

  1. Use Free CRM to capture residual conversion, not as the default. Make the Starter and Pro trials the main signup path again, and move trialists who don't buy to Free CRM when the trial ends (a reverse trial). Trials convert to paid at ~29% vs 4.7% for Free CRM, and ~71% of trialists don't buy today; Free CRM gives them a reason to stay instead of leaving. Validate it against today's setup on paying customers per visitor over 90 days, so Free CRM's slower upgrades are counted.
  2. Use in-product messaging to upsell Free CRM users to Starter in their first month. Every Free CRM upgrade today happens within 29 days of signup, and all of them go to Starter. Connect upgrade prompts to the moments when users hit a Free CRM feature limit, so the message shows up when the value of Starter is most obvious.
  3. Add a product adoption layer alongside the purchase flow. Track a few activation events for every signup, trial or free (for example: contacts imported, first deal created, a teammate invited). This tells us whether product adoption changed with the new packaging, why ~95% of Free CRM users don't upgrade (they don't adopt, or they adopt but have no reason to pay), and later which trialists are worth moving to Free CRM.

Sequencing. Start 1 and 3 right away. Tracking adoption is critical for any free or freemium product, and nothing else depends on it to start. The reverse trial forces Free CRM to generate incremental value while we learn its long-term effect, and running it as a test limits the downside on paying customers. Recommendation 2 needs the adoption data from 3 to know when to trigger the upgrade message, so it follows once that data exists.

3. How much of the drop each factor explains

We split the change in monthly paying customers into three parts (Kitagawa decomposition): volume (more signups), mix (which SKU they sign up for) and rate (conversion inside each SKU). The three parts add up exactly to the total change.

Assumption: Free CRM didn't exist in Q1, so it has no Q1 conversion rate. We use its Q2 rate for both periods, which puts its whole effect in mix. Query: 03_decomposition.sql.

4. The drop is the same for every type of signup

If traffic quality were the cause, we'd expect the drop to be concentrated in some channels and to build gradually as Display grew from February. Instead it hits every slice at once, in April. Free CRM also takes 65–74% of Q2 signups everywhere, including visitors who land on the Starter trial page (67%) and on pricing (68%).

The gaps between slices that existed in Q1 are still there in Q2: small companies convert better than large ones, Email and Social above average, tablet below. Those are structural differences between sources, and they didn't change with the launch. Monthly detail for every slice is in Appendix B4.

Appendix A — Glossary

  • Session: one visit to the Northwind website.
  • Signup: a lead registering for one SKU: Starter trial, Pro trial or Free CRM.
  • SKU: the product a lead signs up for (Starter trial, Pro trial or Free CRM). Not the plan they end up buying: Free CRM upgrades are bought as Starter.
  • Win / paying customer: a signup whose opportunity closed with an amount above $0 within 30 days of signing up. $0 deals (comps, coupons, internal) don't count.
  • Signup → paid conversion: wins divided by signups, for the same group of signups.
  • Blended conversion: signup → paid across all SKUs together.
  • Cohort: signups grouped by the week or month they signed up. A cohort's wins are counted whenever they close, up to 30 days later.
  • Q1 / Q2: Jan–Mar 2026 (before Free CRM) and Apr–Jun 2026 (after launch), compared as monthly averages.
  • QoQ: change from the Q1 monthly average to the Q2 monthly average.
  • Mix shift: a change in the share of signups going to each SKU.
  • Deal value: the opportunity amount: $35 for Starter, $110 for Pro.
